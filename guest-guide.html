Welcome to Westpunt

This guide covers everything you need to know for your stay at Blue Tide. We've curated our favorite beaches, restaurants, snorkel spots, and essential information to help you experience the real Curaçao. Bookmark this page—we update it regularly as we discover new gems.

Know Before You Go

Essential information to make your trip smooth and enjoyable.

Packing Essentials

  • Reef-safe sunscreen – Protects coral reefs. Look for Raw Elements, Stream2Sea, Badger, or Thrive. Non-reef-safe sunscreen kills coral.
  • Snorkel gear – Worth buying before you arrive. Rental gear at beaches doesn't always fit well, and fins make a huge difference—you'll cover more distance with less effort.
  • Water shoes – Many beaches have rocky coral entries. These make a big difference.
  • Bug spray – Mosquitoes are active at dawn and dusk, especially near water.
  • Reusable water bottle – Tap water is safe to drink.
  • Brimmed hat – The sun is strong year-round. Essential for beach days and outdoor activities.
  • Waterproof phone case – Useful for marine life photos and keeping your phone safe at the beach. An inexpensive lanyard-style pouch works fine.
  • Adapters – Blue Tide has 220V European-style outlets (Type F). Most modern electronics (phones, laptops) are dual-voltage and will work fine. We have two US-to-EU adapters available, but bring your own if you have multiple US devices.

Money & Currency

Currency: Caribbean guilder (XCG), introduced March 31, 2025. Pegged to USD at 1.79:1 (same as the old Netherlands Antillean guilder it replaced).

USD accepted: Most places accept USD. You'll get change in guilders.

Credit cards & Apple Pay: Accepted nearly everywhere. You may never need cash during your stay.

ATMs: Hard to find in Westpunt (nearest are 15-20 minutes toward Willemstad). Withdraw guilders if you need cash.

Tipping: Service charge (10-15%) is usually included in your bill. Check before adding extra.

Driving in Curaçao

Drive on the right – Same as the US.

Speed limits – Rarely posted. Typically 80 km/h (50 mph) on highways, 40-60 km/h (25-37 mph) through towns. Slow down in villages.

Rental car essential – Public transport is limited. Support local companies like Blue Lobster or Dushi rather than global chains. Book ahead during peak season.

Gas stations – Prices are fixed island-wide. Pay before pumping or leave your card with the attendant to fill your tank.

Watch for iguanas, goats, donkeys, and deer – They cross roads without looking. Common in rural areas, especially at night.

Weather & Climate

Year-round temperature: 78-88°F (26-31°C). Water stays warm regardless of season.

Rain: Weather apps show daily rain icons—ignore them. Curaçao is arid. Most "showers" are 5-minute tropical bursts, often overnight. Rarely impacts plans.

Rainy season: October-January sees slightly more rain, mostly at night or early morning. Days are still sunny.

Outside hurricane belt: Unlike most of the Caribbean, Curaçao is virtually never affected by hurricanes. Plan fall/summer trips without storm concerns.

Trade winds: Constant easterly breeze keeps it comfortable. Paradoxically, when hurricanes pass to the north, Curaçao can experience calmer winds and hotter temperatures.

Language

Official languages: Dutch and Papiamentu

English: Widely spoken in tourist areas and by younger generations

Spanish: Commonly understood—many locals speak Spanish from exposure to Venezuelan and Colombian TV and radio

Useful Papiamentu phrases:

  • Bon dia (bon DEE-ah) – Good morning
  • Bon tardi (bon TAR-dee) – Good afternoon
  • Bon nochi (bon NO-chee) – Good evening/night
  • Danki (DAN-kee) – Thank you
  • Con ta bai? (kon ta BYE) – How are you?
  • Mi ta bon (mee ta BON) – I'm good

Safety

Overall: Curaçao is very safe. Use common sense as you would anywhere.

Ocean safety: Be mindful of currents when swimming beyond sheltered coves. Use the buddy system when snorkeling.

Wildlife: Iguanas are harmless—don't feed them. Don't approach wild pigs if you see them at beaches. Leave marine life undisturbed. Watch for goats, donkeys, and deer on roads, especially at night.

Vehicle safety: Don't leave valuables in your car at beaches. Many locals leave doors unlocked to show there's nothing inside.

Drinking water: Tap water is distilled seawater and excellent to drink—no need to buy bottled water.

Beaches

All within easy reach from Blue Tide Westpunt.

Walking Distance

Playa Piskadó (Playa Grandi)

5 min walk

"Turtle Beach." Local fishermen clean their catch here daily; sea turtles show up for scraps. Best time: 7-9am. Bring snorkel gear.

No facilities. Rocky entry.

5-15 Minutes Drive

Playa Forti

5 min drive

Famous for the 40-foot cliff jump from the restaurant above. Dark coarse sand, rugged feel. The clifftop restaurant (also called Playa Forti) has excellent sunset views.

Rocky entry.

Playa Jeremi

7 min drive

Secluded narrow cove carved into cliffs. No facilities, no crowds, no entrance fee. Exceptionally clear water. Popular with local divers. Perfect for a quiet sunset.

No facilities. Bring everything.

Playa Kalki

8 min drive

World-class shore dive and snorkel site nicknamed "Alice in Wonderland." Vibrant coral, abundant marine life. Dive shop on-site (Bahia Apartments & Diving) for gear rental.

Rocky entry but worth it.

Grote Knip (Playa Kenepa Grandi)

10 min drive

Curaçao's most photographed beach. White sand, turquoise water, dramatic cliffs. Cliff jumping on the left side (check depth first). Gets crowded weekends—arrive before 10am.

Small snack bar, restrooms, showers. Free parking (limited). Snorkeling good on the right side near rocks.

Klein Knip (Playa Kenepa Chiki)

12 min drive

Quieter sister beach to Grote Knip. Excellent snorkeling, healthy coral. Shallower and calmer.

No facilities—bring everything. Tight parking—arrive early on weekends.

Playa Lagun

15 min drive

Protected cove, perfect for beginner snorkelers. Frequent dolphin sightings in the bay. Calm, shallow water. Sea turtles common. Small fishing village vibe.

Restaurant on the water (Bahia Beach Bar). Parking tight on weekends.

20-30 Minutes Drive (worth the trip)

Cas Abou

25 min drive

Consistently ranked among the Caribbean's best beaches. White sand, palm trees, full amenities: massages, snack bar, showers, restrooms, palapas for rent. Resort feel. Great for a full day of lounging. Easy snorkeling.

Entrance fee: ~$5.

Playa Porto Mari

30 min drive

Famous for "double reef" snorkeling and wild pigs that occasionally lounge on the sand. Large boardwalk, restaurant, bar. More developed than Westpunt beaches but still beautiful. Good balance of nature and comfort.

Entrance fee: ~$3.50.

Restaurants

Local spots where you'll get great food and probably meet the owners.

Sol Food

5 min drive

Weekend pizza and fresh seafood on a breezy deck. Run by Sunshine and David—you'll probably chat with them. Go for sunset. Bring cash.

Open: Friday-Sunday. Cash only. Reservations recommended on weekends.

Jaanchie's Restaurant

8 min drive

5-generation family restaurant serving traditional Curaçaoan food. Try the goat stew (kabritu stoba) or fresh fish. Ask Jaanchie about island history—he loves to talk.

Closed Mondays. Cash preferred. Lunch and dinner.

Playa Forti

12 min drive

Clifftop restaurant with stunning ocean views. Watch cliff divers jump from 30 feet while you eat fresh catch. Touristy but the view and food justify it.

Open daily. Credit cards accepted. Go before sunset for the best light.

Snorkeling & Diving

Westpunt is known for some of the best snorkeling and diving in the Caribbean.

Best Shore Snorkel Sites

  • Punta Azul (your community!): Walk down the steps and you're in. Healthy coral, parrotfish, angelfish, barracuda, octopus, and occasional turtles.
  • Playa Kalki: "Alice in Wonderland" dive site. World-class shore entry.
  • Playa Piskado: Sea turtles guaranteed (especially 7-9am).
  • Playa Lagun: Calm, protected cove. Great for beginners.
  • Grote Knip: Snorkel the right side near the rocks.

Dive Centers & Gear Rental

Bahia Apartments & Diving (at Playa Kalki) – Gear rental, guided dives, certifications. Professional operation.

Go West Diving (Westpunt area) – Boat dives, shore dives, instruction.

Bring your own gear if possible. Rental masks don't always fit well. Most dive shops also rent snorkel sets (~$10-15/day).

What You'll See

Common species: Parrotfish, angelfish, trumpetfish, barracuda, squid, octopus, moray eels, stingrays, spotted eagle rays, green sea turtles, hawksbill turtles.

Coral: Brain coral, elkhorn coral, sea fans, sponges. The reefs here are healthy—Curaçao has strong marine protection laws.

Parks & Nature

Westpunt puts you minutes from Curaçao's best natural areas.

Christoffel National Park

15 min drive

The hike: Summit Mount Christoffel (375m / 1,220ft) for 360° island views. The top is a scramble, but worth it. CRITICAL: You must start before 10am due to heat—the trail closes after that.

Scenic drives: Two paved routes (Mountain Route and North Coast Route) wind through the park. You'll see native white-tailed deer, wild orchids, and cacti from your air-conditioned car.

Entrance fee: ~$15. Open daily.

Shete Boka National Park

20 min drive

Dramatic north coast where the ocean crashes into limestone cliffs.

Boka Tabla: Walk into a sea cave where massive waves thunder against the rocks. It's powerful.

Boka Pistol: Named for the cannon-like blast created when waves fire into a narrow inlet.

Trails: Two marked coastal paths (Boka Kalki and Boka Wandomi) along the bluffs. Otherworldly landscape.

Entrance fee: ~$10. Wear sturdy shoes.

Hòfi Mango

25 min drive

The mango grove: 300-year-old plantation with 1,100+ mango trees. Cool, lush escape from the sun.

Stairway to Heaven: Panoramic viewpoint with the massive "Hand of God" sculpture facing Mount Christoffel.

History: Historic sugar mill and distillery site. Peaceful blend of nature and heritage.

Restaurant on-site (Kultura Kòrsou). Entrance fee: ~$12.

Savonet Museum

At Christoffel entrance

Restored plantation house with indoor exhibits and outdoor ruins. Tells the story of Curaçao's Arawak, colonial, and post-emancipation history. The grounds alone are worth exploring.

Museo Tula (Landhuis Kenepa)

Near Grote Knip

Just down the road from Westpunt, at the entrance to Grote Knip. Dedicated to the 1795 slave revolt led by Tula. Powerful site with stunning terrace views.

Grocery & Essentials

Stock up when you arrive—there's nothing walkable in Westpunt.

Grocery Stores

Centrum Supermarket (20 min toward Willemstad) – Good selection, reasonable prices. Stock up here on your way from the airport.

Van den Tweel Supermarket (Willemstad, 45 min) – Largest selection, imported goods.

Cost U Less (Near airport, 40 min) – Wholesale club, good for bulk items and American brands.

What to Buy

  • Drinking water (or use tap water—it's safe)
  • Breakfast items, snacks, coffee
  • Fresh produce from local markets (better quality than supermarkets)
  • Reef-safe sunscreen (expensive here—bring from home if possible)
  • Bug spray
  • Ice (for coolers if you're beach hopping)

ATMs & Gas

ATMs: Centrum Supermarket area, or closer to Willemstad.

Gas stations: Nearest is 15-20 minutes from Westpunt. Fill up when you go grocery shopping.
